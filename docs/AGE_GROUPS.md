# Age groups

The checker uses seven age groups. This page says where each boundary comes from and what is an estimate.

| Group | Ages | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Newborn | 0 to 28 days | WHO: the newborn (neonatal) period is the first 28 days of life |
| Infant | 29 days to 11 months | WHO and UN IGME: the post-neonatal period is 28 days to under 1 year |
| Young child | 1 to 4 years | WHO reports children under 5 years as one group |
| Child | 5 to 9 years | Fills the gap before WHO's adolescent range; "child" has no single international age range |
| Adolescent | 10 to 19 years | WHO: adolescence is ages 10 to 19 |
| Adult | 20 to 59 years | Ends where WHO's older-people reference age begins |
| Older adult | 60 years and over | WHO uses 60 as its reference age for older people |

Sources (read on 30 October 2026):

- WHO, Newborn mortality fact sheet: https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/newborn-mortality
- WHO, Child mortality (under 5 years) fact sheet: https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/child-mortality-under-5-years
- WHO, Adolescent health: https://www.who.int/health-topics/adolescent-health
- WHO, Ageing and health fact sheet: https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/ageing-and-health
- Our World in Data, how statistical organizations define age periods in children: https://ourworldindata.org/how-do-statistical-organizations-define-age-periods-in-children

## Newborn danger signs (sourced)

The WHO newborn mortality fact sheet lists these signs that need prompt care: feeding problems, reduced activity, difficult breathing, fever, fits or convulsions, and a baby who feels cold. For a newborn, any of them triggers the emergency alert. They map to these checklist items: fever / high fever, fast breathing, difficulty breathing and seizures (already danger signs), and three baby-only items: feeding poorly, much less active, feels cold.

## Estimates (not published figures)

- **Infants (29 days to 11 months):** the three baby-only items raise the advice to "urgent". The WHO page is about newborns; extending this to infants is a cautious design choice.
- **Age multipliers:** how much an age group raises or lowers a disease's score. The earlier "child" figures (ages 0 to 12) were carried over to newborn, infant, young child and child. Adolescent is set halfway between child and adult. Adult and older keep their earlier values; "older" now begins at 60 instead of 65. These are estimates and should be reviewed by a clinician.
- **Pregnancy question:** shown for adolescent girls and adult women.

## Known limits

- Disease text does not yet describe how a disease looks different in a newborn, an adolescent or an older adult.
- A few diseases are clearly rare in some age groups (for example measles in the first months of life) but the inherited figures do not capture that yet.
